What it tells you

What a customs broker names in ten seconds, organized and sourced.

Regime

Courier, small parcel, general cargo, temporary import, samples, compassionate use. Which one suits you and why, with the alternative next to it.

Classification and cost

Mercosur tariff code (NCM) as far as your description allows, which fact decides the rest, and import duties and taxes once every component is confirmed.

Interventions and risks

Whether ANMAT, SENASA, INAL, ENACOM or electrical-safety certification applies. If the goods are dangerous, their class and the governing rules (IATA or IMDG).

It doesn't make things up. When it doesn't know, it says so.

Every fact in the report carries a seal. Anything it cannot confirm with an official source is not asserted: it is flagged, and you are told who decides it or how to request deeper research.

What it covers today

Argentina. Full import coverage; export with guidance everywhere and confirmation where the base already reaches. We state the edge before you find it.

In depth, with sources
  • Full Mercosur tariff nomenclature, as far as your description allows
  • Import duties, statistics fee, VAT and advance tax withholdings
  • Courier and small-parcel regimes (limits, exclusions, exports)
  • General regime: importer/exporter registration and general cargo
  • Lithium batteries: packing instructions and applicable section
Guides and refers
  • Every special regime: what it is, when it applies, who handles it
  • Interventions by ANMAT, SENASA, INAL, ENACOM and electrical safety
  • Dangerous goods: class, UN number and governing rules
  • Anything outside is named as possible and referred, never asserted

Coverage grows with what users ask for: each topic researched once moves from guidance to confirmation. Comexius does not replace your customs broker; it lets you reach them with the homework done.

Who's behind it

Comexius is built by a small team in Buenos Aires with a background in customs brokerage and international logistics. It comes from a concrete frustration: the information about Argentine foreign trade exists, but it is scattered across decrees, resolutions and annexes that nobody starting out can read.

The idea is that anyone can reach their customs broker, their forwarder or their first shipment knowing what to ask. During the beta, every report is also reviewed by a person.
